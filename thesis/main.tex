% Abstract Page
\chapter*{Abstract}

% Add your abstract here
This graduation project explores adversarial attacks and defenses in deep learning across two domains: computer vision and language models. We investigate how adversarial examples can affect image classification, object detection, segmentation, text classification, large language models, and automatic speech recognition. This project also evaluates state‑of‑the‑art defense methods to enhance model robustness, aiming to improve the security and reliability of AI systems in critical applications. To achieve these objectives, we begin with a systematic survey of adversarial research to identify common vulnerability patterns and to establish a conceptual framework for evaluating model integrity. Using widely adopted benchmark datasets, we train representative convolutional and transformer‑based architectures under standard conditions to establish baseline performance metrics. We then deploy a controlled adversarial evaluation regime, measuring the degradation of key indicators such as accuracy, precision, recall, and stability across both vision and language tasks.

In parallel, we generate a diverse set of adversarial samples to characterize how different threat scenarios undermine model predictions, examining the influence of perturbation magnitude and access assumptions on attack success. Based on these insights, we evaluate a suite of defense approaches—ranging from robust training and input transformations to lightweight monitoring mechanisms—testing each for its ability to recover performance without introducing prohibitive latency or resource overhead. We quantify the trade‑offs between security gains and operational cost, and integrate the most effective techniques into a modular safeguard system that adapts its protections in response to detected attack patterns. Our results demonstrate balanced advances in both attack understanding and defense effectiveness, yielding a practical blueprint for deploying resilient, trustworthy AI solutions in safety‑critical settings.
