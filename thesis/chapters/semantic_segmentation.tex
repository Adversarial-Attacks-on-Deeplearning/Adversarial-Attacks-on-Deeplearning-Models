\section{Introduction}

Image segmentation is the process of partitioning a digital image into multiple segments (sets of pixels) to simplify or change the representation of an image into something more meaningful and easier to analyze. It plays a critical role in various computer vision tasks such as medical imaging, autonomous driving, and scene understanding, by enabling the identification and delineation of objects within an image.

\subsection{U-Net Architecture}
The U-Net \cite{ronneberger2015u} is a convolutional neural network architecture originally designed for biomedical image segmentation. Its encoder--decoder structure with skip connections allows it to capture both context and fine-grained localization:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Encoder (contracting) path:} Repeated application of two 3×3 convolutions (with padding to preserve spatial dimensions), each followed by a ReLU activation and a 2×2 max pooling for down-sampling. At each down-sampling step, the number of feature channels doubles.
\item \textbf{Decoder (expanding) path:} Each step consists of an up-convolution (2×2) that halves the number of feature channels, concatenation with the corresponding feature map from the encoder via skip connections, followed by two 3×3 convolutions and ReLU activations.
\end{itemize}
The only modification in our implementation from the original U-Net is the use of padding in every convolutional layer to maintain feature-map size; otherwise, the structure remains identical. For completeness, a high-level view of our PyTorch implementation is available in our GitHub repository: \url{https://github.com/Adversarial-Attacks-on-Deeplearning/U_net}.

\subsection{Accuracy Metrics for Segmentation} Segmentation performance is evaluated using pixel-wise overlap metrics and region-based scores. In our implementation, we encapsulate the computation of pixel accuracy and Dice score in the following algorithmic form:

\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Compute Pixel Accuracy and Dice Score}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require trained segmentation model, data loader yielding (image, mask) pairs
\Ensure overall pixel accuracy and average Dice score

\State Initialize counters:
\[
  \textit{correct\_pixels} \gets 0,\quad
  \textit{total\_pixels} \gets 0,\quad
  \textit{dice\_accumulator} \gets 0
\]
\For{each batch of images and masks}
  \State Predict masks using the model
  \State Binarize predictions (threshold at 0.5)
  \State Count matching pixels in batch:
    \[
      \textit{batch\_correct} \gets \#\{p \mid \text{pred}(p) = \text{gt}(p)\}
    \]
  \State Determine batch size (total pixels):
    \[
      \textit{batch\_pixels} \gets \#\{\text{pixels in batch}\}
    \]
  \State Compute batch Dice score:
    \[
      \textit{batch\_dice}
      \;=\;\frac{2 \times \lvert \text{pred} \cap \text{gt}\rvert}
               {\lvert \text{pred}\rvert + \lvert \text{gt}\rvert + \epsilon}
    \]
  \State Update accumulators:
    \begin{align*}
      \textit{correct\_pixels}   &\gets \textit{correct\_pixels} + \textit{batch\_correct},\\
      \textit{total\_pixels}     &\gets \textit{total\_pixels}   + \textit{batch\_pixels},\\
      \textit{dice\_accumulator} &\gets \textit{dice\_accumulator} + \textit{batch\_dice}
    \end{align*}
\EndFor
\State Compute final metrics:
\[
  \textit{pixel\_accuracy}
  = \frac{\textit{correct\_pixels}}{\textit{total\_pixels}},\quad
  \textit{average\_dice}
  = \frac{\textit{dice\_accumulator}}{\textit{number\_of\_batches}}
\]
\Return pixel\_accuracy, average\_dice
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

\noindent Here, \textit{pixel accuracy} is the percentage of correctly classified pixels, and the \textit{Dice score} (Sørensen--Dice coefficient) quantifies overlap between predicted and ground truth masks.

\noindent Here, \textit{pixel accuracy} is computed as the ratio of correctly predicted pixels to the total number of pixels, and the \textit{Dice score} (Sørensen--Dice coefficient) is calculated per batch and averaged over all batches.

\subsubsection{FGSM Attack on UNet}

\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
The Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) attack is applied to UNet model to evaluate its robustness by degrading segmentation performance. FGSM introduces $\ell_\infty$-bounded perturbations, controlled by $\epsilon$, to input images, causing reduced accuracy and Dice scores compared to ground truth masks, often resulting in incorrect segmentations or missed regions. This attack preserves visual similarity to the original image while disrupting the model’s predictions, highlighting vulnerabilities in critical applications like medical imaging.

\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\

\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM) Attack for UNet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $model$: Trained UNet model, $Data$: (image, mask) pairs, $\epsilon$: Perturbation magnitude
\Ensure $adversarial\_image$: Adversarial image batch, normalized to [0, 1]

\State Initialize loss function: $loss\_fn \gets \text{BCEWithLogitsLoss}()$
\State Set $model$ to evaluation mode

\State Initialize $adversarial\_image \gets image.clone().detach()$
\State Enable gradient computation: $adversarial\_image.requires\_grad \gets \text{True}$

\State Compute prediction: $prediction \gets model(adversarial\_image)$
\State Compute loss: $loss \gets loss\_fn(prediction, mask.unsqueeze(1))$
\State Backpropagate: $loss.backward()$

\State Extract gradient: $grad \gets adversarial\_image.grad$
\State Update image: $adversarial\_image \gets adversarial\_image + \epsilon \cdot \text{sign}(grad)$
\State Clip to valid range: $adversarial\_image \gets \text{clamp}(adversarial\_image, 0, 1)$

\State Detach $adversarial\_image$ from computation graph
\State \Return $adversarial\_image$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

\subsubsection{PGD Attack on UNet}

\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
The Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) \cite{madry2018towardspgd} attack is applied to the UNet model to evaluate its robustness by degrading segmentation performance. Unlike the single-step Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), which applies a one-time perturbation, PGD iteratively refines perturbations using gradient updates and projects them onto an $\ell_\infty$-bounded ball, achieving stronger disruptions. This results in reduced accuracy and Dice scores compared to ground truth masks, often causing incorrect segmentations or missed regions. PGD preserves visual similarity to the original image while significantly disrupting the model’s predictions.

\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\

\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Projected Gradient Descent (PGD) Attack for UNet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $model$: Trained UNet model, $data$: Data loader yielding (image, mask) pairs, $\epsilon$: Perturbation magnitude, $\alpha$: Step size, $iterations$: Number of iterations
\Ensure $adversarial\_image$: Adversarial image batch, normalized to [0, 1]

\State Initialize loss function: $loss\_fn \gets \text{BCEWithLogitsLoss}()$
\State Set $model$ to evaluation mode

\State Initialize $adversarial\_image \gets image.clone().detach()$
\For{$i \gets 1$ to $iterations$}
    \State Compute prediction: $prediction \gets model(adversarial\_image)$
    \State Compute loss: $loss \gets loss\_fn(prediction, mask)$
    \State Backpropagate: $loss.backward()$
    
    \State Update image: $adversarial\_image \gets adversarial\_image + \alpha \cdot \text{sign}(adversarial\_image.grad)$
    \State Project to $\epsilon$-ball: $adversarial\_image \gets \max(\min(adversarial\_image, original\_image + \epsilon), original\_image - \epsilon)$
    \State Clip to valid range: $adversarial\_image \gets \text{clamp}(adversarial\_image, 0, 1)$
\EndFor

\State \Return $adversarial\_image$

\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

The PGD attack was applied with the maximum allowable perturbation  $\epsilon$=0.031 and 0.062 with the step size used for each gradient update $\alpha$=2/255 for 10 iterations. \subsubsection{MI-FGSM Attack on UNet}
\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
Momenum Iterative Fast Gradient Signed Method (MI-FGSM) \cite{dong2018boostingadversarialattacksmomentum} is a technique for accelerating gradient descent algorithms by accumulating a velocity vector in the gradient direction of the loss function across iterations. The memorization of previous gradients helps to barrel through narrow valleys, small humps and poor local minima or maxima. The momentum method also shows its effectiveness in stochastic gradient descent to stabilize the updates. For UNet, this method can systematically introduce small, human-imperceptible perturbations to input images, to assess how these alterations affect segmentation accuracy and Dice scores. By integrating momentum with a decay factor, MI-FGSM can effectively target UNet's decision boundaries, providing insights into its robustness attack scenarios.

\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\

\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Momentum Iterative Fast Gradient Sign Method (MI-FGSM) Attack for UNet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $model$: Trained UNet model, $loader$: Data loader yielding (image, mask) pairs, $\epsilon$: Perturbation magnitude, $iterations$: Number of iterations, $\mu$: Momentum decay factor
\Ensure $adversarial\_image$: Adversarial image batch, normalized to [0, 1]

\State Compute step size: $\alpha \gets \epsilon / iterations$
\State Initialize $adversarial\_image \gets image.clone().detach()$
\State Initialize momentum: $g \gets \text{zeros\_like}(adversarial\_image)$

\For{$t \gets 1$ to $iterations$}
    \State Compute prediction: $prediction \gets model(adversarial\_image)$
    \State Compute loss: $loss \gets loss\_fn(prediction, mask)$
    \State Backpropagate: $loss.backward()$
    
    \State Update momentum: $g \gets \mu \cdot g + adversarial\_image.grad$
    \State Update image: $adversarial\_image \gets adversarial\_image + \alpha \cdot \text{sign}(g)$
    \State Project to $\epsilon$-ball: $adversarial\_image \gets \max(\min(adversarial\_image, original\_image + \epsilon), original\_image - \epsilon)$
    \State Clip to valid range: $adversarial\_image \gets \text{clamp}(adversarial\_image, 0, 1)$
   
\EndFor

\State \Return $adversarial\_image$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

\subsubsection{JSMA Attack on UNet}

\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
The Jacobian-based Saliency Map Attack (JSMA) \cite{papernot2016limitations} leverages the forward derivative of the deep neural network (DNN) to construct adversarial saliency maps, identifying the most influential input features to perturb for misclassification. Originally designed for classification tasks, JSMA aims to minimize total distortion, defined as the number of perturbed pixels, making it more suitable for scenarios where few pixel changes suffice. In our work, we adapted JSMA to a UNet image segmentation model, despite challenges, as achieving a targeted mask in segmentation often requires modifying many pixels, leading to 150–200 iterations for notable accuracy degradation.


\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\
To enhance performance and speed, we compute the element-wise gradients of the sigmoid output with respect to the input image, defined as:
\[
\text{grad}_{i,j} = \frac{\partial F_{i,j}}{\partial x_{i,j}}
\]
where \(\text{grad}_{i,j}\) shares the same shape as the input image \(X\), representing the sensitivity of each output pixel to input changes. This approach effectively perturbs object boundaries, boosting attack success. The adversarial saliency map, derived from the Jacobian, identifies pixels to modify, guided by a target mask to align gradients with desired segmentation outcomes.


\paragraph{Threat Model}
We employ a targeted misclassification attack, forcing the UNet to predict specific pixels as class 1 or 0 according to the target mask. While this adaptation is less efficient than in classification due to the extensive pixel modifications required, the use of element-wise gradients enhances the attack’s ability to disrupt segmentation accuracy.

\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Jacobian-based Saliency Map Attack (JSMA) for UNet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $model$: Trained UNet model, $image$: Input image [C, H, W], $target\_label$: Desired output [H, W], $\gamma$: Max distortion, $\theta$: Step size, $\epsilon$: Max pixel distortion, $N$: Num. pixels to perturb per itr.
\Ensure $adversarial$: Adversarial image [C, H, W]

\State $adversarial \gets image.clone().detach()$
\While{$distortion < \gamma$}
    \State $grads \gets \text{compute\_output\_grads}(model, adversarial)$
    \State $saliency \gets \text{saliency\_map}(grads, target\_label)$
    \State $top\_indices \gets \text{topk}(\text{max}(saliency, N).indices$
    \For{$idx$ in $top\_indices$}
        \State $(i, j) \gets \text{unravel\_index}(idx, (H, W))$
        \State $adversarial[:, i, j] \gets adversarial[:, i, j] + \theta \cdot \text{sign}(saliency[:, i, j])$
        \State $adversarial \gets \max(\min(adversarial, image + \epsilon), image - \epsilon)$
        \State $adversarial \gets \text{clamp}(adversarial, 0, 1)$
    \EndFor
    \State $distortion \gets (adversarial \neq image).sum().float() / \text{numel}(image)$
\EndWhile
\State \Return $adversarial$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

\subsubsection{Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP) Attack on U-Net Segmentation Model}

% Introducing the UAP attack on U-Net
\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
Now we will explore the vulnerability of U-Net, to Universal Adversarial Perturbation (UAP) attacks\cite{moosavi2017universal}.
% Outlining methodology with algorithm
\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\
The methodology involves training a U-Net model for medical image segmentation and subsequently generating a UAP to disrupt its performance. The U-Net architecture, comprising an encoder-decoder structure with skip connections, is implemented with double convolutional layers, batch normalization, and ReLU activations. The UAP is crafted using a gradient-based optimization process to maximize segmentation error while constraining perturbation magnitude.

% Presenting the UAP training algorithm
\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Universal Adversarial Perturbation Training for U-Net}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $X = \{x_1, \dots, x_N\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$: training images, $M$: U-Net model, $Y = \{y_1, \dots, y_N\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times K}$: ground truth masks, $E > 0$: number of epochs, $\epsilon > 0$: perturbation bound, $\alpha > 0$: learning rate, $\beta > 0$: momentum, $\ell$: BCEWithLogitsLoss
\Ensure $\delta \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$: universal perturbation
\State Preprocess each $x_i \in X$ for input to $M$ \Comment{E.g., normalize to $[0, 1]$, resize to $160 \times 240$}
\State Initialize $\delta \gets 0$ \Comment{Set initial perturbation to zero}
\State Initialize optimizer: $\text{opt} \gets \text{SGD}(\{\delta\}, \text{lr}=\alpha, \text{momentum}=\beta)$
\For{$e \gets 1$ to $E$}
    \State Initialize epoch loss: $L_e \gets 0$
    \For{batch $\{x_i, y_i\}$ in $X, Y$}
        \State Compute perturbed images: $x_{adv} \gets \text{clamp}(x_i + \delta, 0, 1)$
        \State Compute predictions: $p \gets M(x_{adv})$ \Comment{$p \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times K}$: segmentation logits}
        \State Compute loss: $L \gets -\ell(p, y_i)$ \Comment{Maximize segmentation error}
        \State Accumulate epoch loss: $L_e \gets L_e + L$
        \State Compute gradient: $g \gets \nabla_{\delta} L$ \Comment{Gradient w.r.t. $\delta$}
        \State Update perturbation: $\text{opt.step()}$ \Comment{SGD update with momentum}
        \State Clamp perturbation: $\delta \gets \text{clip}(\delta, -\epsilon, \epsilon)$ \Comment{Ensure $||\delta||_\infty \leq \epsilon$}
    \EndFor
    \State Compute average epoch loss: $L_e \gets L_e / N$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\delta$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

% Detailing implementation specifics
\subparagraph{Implementation Details}\leavevmode\\
The UAP attack is implemented with the following parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input Size}: $160 \times 240$ pixels.
    \item \textbf{Batch Size}: 4, balancing gradient diversity and memory efficiency to enhance perturbation universality.
    \item \textbf{Perturbation Bound} ($\epsilon$): 0.2, empirically chosen to ensure imperceptibility.
    \item \textbf{Training Epochs}: 7, sufficient for convergence.
    \item \textbf{Learning Rate} ($\alpha$): 0.1, empirically optimized.
    \item \textbf{Momentum} ($\beta$): 0.9, for stable optimization.
    \item \textbf{Loss Function}: BCEWithLogitsLoss, suitable for binary segmentation tasks.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Deep Fool}
\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
Now we investigate the susceptibility of U-Net, to DeepFool adversarial attacks~\cite{moosavi2016deepfool}. DeepFool generates input-specific perturbations that exploit the decision boundaries of the model to minimally perturb each image, causing missegmentation while maintaining imperceptibility.

% Outlining methodology with algorithm
\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\
The methodology involves applying the DeepFool attack to a pretrained U-Net model using gradient-based optimization constrained by a small perturbation bound.



\begin{breakablealgorithm}{DeepFool Attack for U-Net}
\label{alg:deepfool_unet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$: input image, $M$: U-Net model, $\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times K}$: ground truth mask, $I > 0$: maximum iterations, $\epsilon > 0$: overshoot bound, $\ell$: BCEWithLogitsLoss, $\texttt{device}$: computation device
\Ensure $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$: adversarial image, $\mathbf{r} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$: perturbation
\State Preprocess $\mathbf{x}$ for input to $M$ \Comment{E.g., normalize to $[0, 1]$, resize to $160 \times 240$}
\State Initialize $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}} \gets \mathbf{x}$, $\mathbf{r} \gets \mathbf{0}$ \Comment{Set initial perturbation to zero}
\State Move $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}}$ to $\texttt{device}$ with $\texttt{requires\_grad}=\text{True}$ \Comment{Enable gradient computation}
\For{$i \gets 1$ to $I$}
    \State Compute predictions: $\mathbf{p} \gets M(\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}})$ \Comment{$\mathbf{p} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times K}$: segmentation logits}
    \State Compute target: $\mathbf{t} \gets (\mathbf{y} > 0.5)$ \Comment{Binary mask as target}
    \State Compute prediction: $\hat{\mathbf{p}} \gets (\text{sigmoid}(\mathbf{p}) > 0.5)$ \Comment{Binary prediction}
    \If{$\hat{\mathbf{p}} = \mathbf{t}$} \Comment{Check if prediction matches target}
        \State \textbf{break} \Comment{Exit if no missegmentation}
    \EndIf
    \State Compute loss: $L \gets \ell(\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{t})$ \Comment{Binary cross-entropy loss}
    \State Compute gradient: $\mathbf{g} \gets \nabla_{\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}}} L$ \Comment{Gradient w.r.t. $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}}$}
    \State Normalize gradient: $\mathbf{w} \gets \mathbf{g} / (\lVert \mathbf{g} \rVert_2 + 10^{-8})$ \Comment{Perturbation direction}
    \State Compute perturbation step: $\mathbf{r}_i \gets (L + 10^{-4}) \cdot \mathbf{w}$ \Comment{Small perturbation}
    \State Update total perturbation: $\mathbf{r} \gets (\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}_i)$ \Comment{Accumulate perturbation}
    \State Clamp perturbation: $\mathbf{r} \gets \texttt{clip}(\mathbf{r}, -\epsilon, \epsilon)$ \Comment{Ensure $\lVert \mathbf{r} \rVert_\infty \leq \epsilon$}
    \State Update adversarial image: $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}} \gets \texttt{clamp}(\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{r}, 0, 1)$ \Comment{Apply perturbation}
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathbf{x}_{\text{adv}}, \mathbf{r}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

% Detailing implementation specifics
\subparagraph{Implementation Details}\leavevmode\\
The DeepFool attack is implemented with the following parameters:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Input Size}: $160 \times 240$ pixels.
    \item \textbf{Batch Size}: Processed individually per image for input-specific perturbation.
    \item \textbf{Overshoot Bound} ($\epsilon$): 0.02, empirically chosen for minimal perceptibility.
    \item \textbf{Maximum Iterations}: 50, sufficient for convergence.
    \item \textbf{Loss Function}: BCEWithLogitsLoss, appropriate for binary segmentation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Adversarial Training for UNet}
\paragraph{Introduction}\leavevmode\\
Adversarial training enhances the robustness of machine learning models by incorporating adversarial examples into the training process, a technique initially explored for small datasets like MNIST and CIFAR-10 (Goodfellow et al., 2014 \cite{goodfellow2015explaining}). Kurakin et al. (2017) \cite{kurakin2017adversarialmachinelearningscale} extended this approach to large-scale models, such as Inception v3 on ImageNet, demonstrating that adversarial training with one-step methods, like the Fast Gradient Sign Method (FGSM), significantly improves resilience against similar attacks. 

\paragraph{Methodology}\leavevmode\\
We adapted adversarial training for a UNet segmentation model to improve its robustness against adversarial perturbations. The training process combines clean and adversarially perturbed images, generated using the FGSM approach with varying perturbation magnitudes. The loss function is a weighted sum, balancing the contribution of clean and adversarial examples:
\[
\text{Loss} = 0.5 \cdot L_{\text{clean}} + 0.2 \cdot L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=8}} + 0.2 \cdot L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=16}} + 0.1 \cdot L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=32}}
\]
where \(L_{\text{clean}}\) is the loss on clean inputs, and \(L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=8}}\), \(L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=16}}\), and \(L_{\text{adv_$\epsilon$=32}}\) are losses on adversarial examples with \(\epsilon = 8/255\), \(16/255\), and \(32/255\), respectively, computed using Binary Cross-Entropy Loss. Adversarial examples are generated by applying the sign of the gradient scaled by \(\epsilon\), followed by clamping to \([0, 1]\).

The training algorithm is outlined as follows:
\begin{breakablealgorithm}{Adversarial Training for UNet}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $loader$: Data loader, $model$: UNet model, $optimizer$: Optimizer, $loss\_fn$: Loss function, $scaler$: Gradient scaler
\Ensure Trained $model$

\For{each batch $(data, targets)$ in $loader$}
    \State Compute clean prediction: $predictions \gets model(data)$
    \State Compute clean loss: $loss\_clean \gets loss\_fn(predictions, targets)$
    \State Backpropagate: $loss\_clean.backward()$
    \State Extract gradient: $grad \gets data.grad$
    \State Generate adversarial examples:
    \State $data\_adv_8 \gets \text{clamp}(data + (8/255) \cdot \text{sign}(grad), 0, 1)$
    \State $data\_adv_{16} \gets \text{clamp}(data + (16/255) \cdot \text{sign}(grad), 0, 1)$
    \State $data\_adv_{32} \gets \text{clamp}(data + (32/255) \cdot \text{sign}(grad), 0, 1)$
    \State Compute adversarial losses:
    \State $loss\_adv_8 \gets loss\_fn(model(data\_adv_8), targets)$
    \State $loss\_adv_{16} \gets loss\_fn(model(data\_adv_{16}), targets)$
    \State $loss\_adv_{32} \gets loss\_fn(model(data\_adv_{32}), targets)$
    \State Compute total loss: $total\_loss \gets 0.5 \cdot loss\_clean + 0.2 \cdot loss\_adv_8 + 0.2 \cdot loss\_adv_{16} + 0.1 \cdot loss\_adv_{32}$
    \State Clear gradients: $optimizer.zero\_grad()$
    \State Scale and backpropagate: $scaler.scale(total\_loss).backward()$
    \State Update weights: $scaler.step(optimizer)$
    \State Update scaler: $scaler.update()$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{breakablealgorithm}

This approach leverages adjusting the loss weighting to balance robustness across different $\epsilon$ values. However, using a fixed $\epsilon$ can limit adaptability, potentially reducing robustness against diverse attack magnitudes and iterative methods. Future work should consider dynamically tuning $\epsilon$ during training to enhance resilience against a broader range of adversarial attacks.
